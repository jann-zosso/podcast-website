Resonance to SO LONG COVID: media coverage, responses and messages

We are deeply moved by the responses, questions and stories that reach us.

SO LONG COVID grew out of a personal recovery story. Since then, we have received many messages from people affected by chronic illness, their families, health professionals and journalists. This page brings together media coverage and a selection of voices from people engaging with the podcast, a neuroplastic understanding of persistent symptoms and the possibility of recovery. Most of the responses shown here were originally written in German or Swiss German and have been translated into English.


Media coverage

Personal accounts

“Your podcast changed my life.”

Dear podcast team, dear fellow sufferers,

Two years ago, as a young, athletic woman, I developed ME/CFS. It kept getting worse, and in the end I spent more than half a year almost entirely in bed and completely dependent on help. I tried countless medications and approaches and researched endlessly until I came across your podcast. Even as a sceptic, it immediately made sense to me. That was four weeks ago. From being bedridden, experiencing the worst crashes and being admitted to hospital, I am now back to a slow-paced everyday life: I exercise at home, read, listen to music and meet friends. Your podcast changed my life. Thank you from the bottom of my heart! 💛

And to everyone who is where I was: Don’t give up! Recovery is possible, and I am well on my way.

- Louise
“So long Covid – so long, migraine!”

I lived with migraine for 40 years. My GPs and neurologists had taught me that migraine was inherited, chronic and incurable. I simply had to live with it with the help of acute medication (triptans). But after treatment, the migraine kept returning at ever shorter intervals. Over the last five years, I had migraine almost every day, eventually every 15–20 hours. So I was taking not only the permitted maximum of 15 triptans per month, but up to 40 triptans per month.

In spring 2026, Tabea told me about the podcast “So Long Covid”. My first thought was: this has nothing to do with me – I don’t have Long Covid. I even forwarded the podcast to an acquaintance with Long Covid without listening to it myself. It was only when she told me about the good progress she was making, and another acquaintance also described the podcast as very helpful, that I listened to two episodes. That was when it clicked for me. For the first time, I was given an explanation of how learned alarm patterns in the brain can also play a role in chronic symptoms – and that we can influence them. This information fundamentally changed how I dealt with 40 years of migraine. For the first time in my life, I had a concrete strategy that convinced me.

At 4 a.m. on 15 May 2026, after another migraine attack, I decided: “Enough is enough! I’m going to stop the medication cold turkey.” Eighteen weeks later, I had not taken a single triptan. Headaches still occurred every two to four days, but most of the time they resolved within one or two hours with breathing exercises and other techniques. The idea of taking a triptan, as I would long since have done in the past, did not even cross my mind.

“So Long Covid” grew out of Jann’s own recovery story. He and his partner, journalist Zita Bauer, turned it into a six-part podcast after extensive research with people affected and experts. For me, the podcast was far more than an interesting story. At exactly the right moment, it gave me the crucial new information and the courage to take a different path.

Thank you, Zita and Jann!

- René
